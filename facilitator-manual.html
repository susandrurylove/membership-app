A Word to the Facilitator

Welcome, dear facilitator. In your hands is more than a deck — it is a map of wisdom hidden inside every cell of the body. Your role is not to fix, diagnose, or advise. It is to hold a sacred container in which each person meets their own embodied wisdom, in the light of reverence, curiosity, and compassion. You guide; their body teaches.

The Core Agreement

Everyone speaks only to their own experience. No advice. No feedback. No fixing. What is shared in the circle is also medicine for the one listening. Confidentiality is honored. Every person's pace is respected — nothing needs to be resolved by dinner time.

Creating Sacred Space

Find at least ten uninterrupted minutes in a quiet place. Light a candle, lay a cloth, or simply take three deep breaths together. Set intention aloud. The more intention you bring, the more receptive the room becomes.

The 3-Card Process (Purpose → Inquiry → Transcendence)

For each card, in order: (1) Read slowly, aloud. (2) Close the eyes; notice sensations, emotions, memories. (3) Place a hand on the body area and say: "I see you. I hear you. Thank you." (4) Write down one thing that stood out. (5) When ready, move to the next card. For Transcendence, speak the statement in first person, as if already true — invite the cells of that part to re-member this as truth.

Facilitating a Circle — Three Rounds

Round 1 · Purpose: each person intuitively chooses a system, reads the Purpose card, shares the title, image, and message aloud with any personal relevance. Others respond in a single sentence of relevance or not.
Round 2 · Inquiry: same format with the Inquiry card.
Round 3 · Transcendence: each shares relevance and any new understanding gained — here each person's wisdom becomes a catalyst for others. Close with group silence, then a round of appreciation. Allow an hour or more, with journaling time between rounds.

Safety & Referral (Non-Negotiable)

These cards support healing; they do not replace professional care. If a participant experiences overwhelming distress, dissociation, panic, or thoughts of self-harm, gently pause the process and direct them to professional support — a licensed therapist, a crisis line (988 in Canada & the US), or the nearest emergency room. Recognizing the need for additional support is itself an act of wisdom. Remind participants: not all healing is expressed on the physical level, and that is okay.
