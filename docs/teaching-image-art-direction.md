# Teaching Image Art Direction

**Purpose:** Give every teaching a unique, emotionally resonant image that directly expresses its subject while keeping the full library unmistakably Susan Drury.

## Shared Visual Language

Every image will be a **cinematic spiritual-editorial nature illustration** with a refined, contemplative finish. The palette will center on midnight navy, deep teal, antique gold, warm ivory, moss, soft rose, and natural earth tones. Light should feel meaningful rather than decorative: dawn glow, filtered woodland light, moonlit water, luminous breath, or subtle golden energy may reveal the teaching’s emotional movement. Images must feel elegant, mature, calm, and credible—not New Age clip art, stock wellness photography, or generic fantasy.

The production aspect ratio is **16:10 landscape**, composed for both library cards and wide article heroes. Each frame needs a strong subject in the central 70 percent, useful darker or quieter edge space for responsive crops, layered depth, and no embedded text, logos, borders, UI, or watermarks.

## Topic-to-Visual Rules

| Teaching domain | Direct visual treatment |
|---|---|
| Body systems | The named body region is the unmistakable focal subject, interpreted through graceful anatomical silhouette, natural metaphor, and supportive surrounding landscape. Avoid clinical gore and false medical diagrams. |
| Origins and early patterns | Thresholds, roots, nests, childhood-scale environments, inherited pathways, layered shadows, and emerging light should express the exact pattern named in the teaching. |
| Transformation and healing | Show a specific transition—opening, thawing, releasing, crossing, mending, rising, or reorganizing—rather than a generic glowing person. |
| Relationships and love | Use relational distance, gesture, paired natural forms, threads, mirrors, bridges, or shared light to express the particular relationship dynamic. |
| Tao and timeless wisdom | Translate the named principle into elemental movement: water, stone, mist, valleys, uncarved wood, empty vessels, pathways, or balanced opposing forces. |
| Meditation and sacred practice | Depict the actual practice object or action—breath, stillness, journaling, visualization, candle, walking, listening, or grounding—with uncluttered contemplative space. |
| Susan’s reflections | Create an editorial scene tied to the article’s concrete place, season, object, event, or central metaphor. Avoid default portraits unless the teaching itself is biographical. |
| Therapeutic perspectives | Show the modality’s real sensory setting or mechanism through humane, non-clinical editorial imagery. Do not imply guaranteed outcomes or depict unsafe treatment. |
| Begin Here | Use welcoming thresholds, first steps, open books, gentle paths, sunrise, and grounded invitation while still incorporating the teaching’s exact subject. |

## Uniqueness System

Each teaching receives a distinct combination of **primary subject, setting, symbolic action, time of day, camera distance, color emphasis, and natural motif**. Adjacent teachings in the same collection must not repeat the same primary subject, setting, or composition. Reused website artwork will be replaced; no two final teaching records may share an image URL or perceptually similar composition.

## People and Representation

When people are relevant, use diverse adults across age, skin tone, body type, and mobility. Poses must be natural and anatomically credible. Faces should be calm and emotionally specific, never uncanny, overly glamorous, sexualized, or devotional toward Susan. Images involving childhood, pregnancy, illness, grief, trauma, or physical symptoms must remain gentle, dignified, non-graphic, and non-diagnostic.

## Acceptance Criteria

An image passes only when a reviewer can infer the teaching’s core topic without reading its title, the composition remains attractive at card and hero crops, it belongs to the shared Susan Drury palette and mood, it is materially different from every other teaching image, and it contains no text, watermark, anatomical distortion, visual artifact, unsafe implication, or unrelated decorative subject.
